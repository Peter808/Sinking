  1. 2026
    Jul 2026In progress

    Shorecrest South flood improvements

    $34.6M

    New and larger storm drains, injection wells, and a stormwater pump station that empties into the Little River Canal, plus rebuilt roads.

    OutcomeBuilt to relieve street flooding for 327 properties worth about $264M. Phase I construction July 2026 to March 2027; Phase II through May 2029.

    Source: City of Miami

  2. 2025
    Nov 2025In design

    Brickell Bay Drive seawall and road

    $32.7M

    New seawall, bay walk, rebuilt road, and drainage along Brickell Bay Drive from SE 14th Street to SE 15th Road.

    OutcomeProtects about 787 homes and $316M in property value from storm surge and rising seas. Construction expected February 2027 to January 2029.

    Source: City of Miami

  3. 2024
    May 2024Planned

    Back Bay storm surge plan, revised

    $2.7B

    The Army Corps of Engineers' redrawn plan drops the floodwall. It elevates about 2,100 homes, floodproofs about 400 critical buildings like fire stations and shelters, and studies reefs and mangroves.

    OutcomeSent to Congress for authorization in 2024. Federally funded if approved; not yet built.

    Source: WUWF / WLRN

  4. 2021
    May 2021Planned

    Miami Stormwater Master Plan

    $3.8B

    A 40-year plan for giant pumps, miles of 6-foot seawalls, thousands of injection wells, and 8-foot underground pipes.

    OutcomeAims to keep Miami livable largely as it is through 2060. The city says it will not keep every neighborhood dry.

    Source: Building Design + Construction

  5. 2020
    Jun 2020Revised

    Back Bay floodwall plan

    $4.6B

    The Army Corps' first plan: a mile-long floodwall along Biscayne Bay and flood gates across the Miami River, Little River, and Biscayne Canal.

    OutcomeRejected by residents and the county. In 2022 the Corps agreed to redo it with more natural protection.

    Source: WLRN, 2020, WLRN, 2022

  6. 2017
    Nov 2017In progress

    Miami Forever Bond

    $192M

    Voters approved a $400M bond. The biggest share, $192M, is set aside for sea level rise and flood prevention.

    OutcomeBy late 2025 only about $30M (16%) of the flood money had been spent. Officials cite delays, lost grants, and changing plans.

    Source: WLRN, 2025, City of Miami
